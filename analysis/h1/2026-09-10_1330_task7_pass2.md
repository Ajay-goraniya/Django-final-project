# H1 — Task 7 pass 2: the confidence gate does NOT ship

**Verdict: refuted at the sample size that counts. Pass 1's headline (+16.36 on 143 EF fires) does
not survive on a larger fire set. Do not gate anything on it. The base-rate findings from pass 1
stand unchanged.**

## 1. The test that decides it — the v10 runner, 182 matched fires

This is the only fire set where the gate clears V's ship rule of **≥100 kept fires** at every
threshold, so it is the one that adjudicates. Model trained only on candles strictly before the
first fire; read at each fire's actual second (p25 36 s, median 68 s, p75 133 s).

| gate | kept | hit | **PnL** | removed | removed record | h1 | h2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **baseline** | 182 | 54% | **+23.57** | 0 | — | +13.20 | **+10.37** |
| conf ≥ 0.40 | 155 | 55% | +21.71 | 27 | 12/15, +1.86 | +15.83 | +5.88 |
| conf ≥ 0.45 | 139 | 58% | **+24.38** | 43 | 18/25, −0.82 | +15.98 | +8.41 |
| conf ≥ 0.50 | 133 | 56% | +20.78 | 49 | 23/26, +2.78 | +14.58 | +6.21 |
| conf ≥ 0.55 | 121 | 58% | +23.46 | 61 | 28/33, +0.11 | +15.97 | +7.49 |

**The gate raises hit rate (54% → 58%) and does not raise PnL.** The best threshold beats baseline
by +0.81 — noise — and two of the four thresholds are *below* baseline. Every threshold makes the
second half worse than baseline's +10.37.

The mechanism is visible in the removed columns: the fires it removes are roughly break-even
(−0.82 to +2.78 across thresholds) despite losing records. **It is removing cheap winners.** A
low-confidence fire bought at 0.30 pays 2.27 when it lands; culling those raises the hit rate and
costs the PnL that pays for the misses. Hit rate and PnL are different objectives here, and the
gate optimises the wrong one.

## 2. Why pass 1 looked better

Pass 1 used the v10 *engine's* 143 EF fires and got conf ≥ 0.55 → 79 kept, 65%, +16.36 vs baseline
+10.80. That result is real for that slice but does not generalise: it kept only 79 fires (below the
bar), its gain was first-half-only (+13.90/+2.47 against baseline's +3.73/+7.08), and on the runner's
larger overlapping set the same construction produces nothing. **I flagged both weaknesses in pass 1
and they turned out to be the whole story. Treat pass 1's table as superseded.**

## 3. REVERSAL — the gate is non-discriminating, for a structural reason

87 of 113 REVERSAL fires matched to klines:

| gate | kept | hit | PnL | removed | h1 | h2 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| baseline | 87 | 67% | +24.79 | 0 | +9.64 | +15.15 |
| conf ≥ 0.55 | 85 | 67% | +25.07 | **2** | +9.64 | +15.43 |
| conf ≥ 0.60 | 83 | 66% | +23.63 | 4 | +8.20 | +15.43 |

At conf ≥ 0.55 it removes **2 fires out of 87**. Nearly every REVERSAL fire already carries high
model confidence in its own side, so the tree has nothing to add: **the tree and REVERSAL's own
entry logic are largely redundant.** My pass-1 suggestion that the gate "belongs on REVERSAL because
AUC is 0.86 there" was wrong — high AUC at that horizon does not help when the lane is already
selecting the same candles. I withdraw it.

## Where this leaves Task 7

The "candle brain" is real as a *description* — the flip probabilities above are solid and they
confirm the user's intuition about multi-reversal candles. What has not worked is turning it into a
**fire/no-fire gate**, because at EF's fire time the model is too weak (AUC 0.61) and the fires it
would cull are the cheap ones that carry the lane's PnL.

Next, in order: (1) wait for 09-10 klines at ~00:00 UTC and re-run with Tokyo's live fills included;
(2) test the gate as a **stake modifier** rather than an on/off switch — size down low-confidence
fires instead of skipping them, which keeps the cheap winners while cutting exposure; (3) test
confidence × entry price jointly, since §1 says the gate's damage is concentrated in cheap fires and
§4 says price is where the real signal is.
