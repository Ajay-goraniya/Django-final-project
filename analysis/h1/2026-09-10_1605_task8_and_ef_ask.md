## The 0.48 ask floor — and why I am not calling it a finding

Skip EF fires quoted below X. Full sweep, both halves, per fire set:

| set | baseline per-fire | baseline halves | **floor 0.48** | halves at 0.48 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| v10 runner (n=279) | +0.096 | +28.81 / **−1.89** | **+0.232** | **+16.21 / +10.74** |
| twin A (n=177) | −0.011 | +0.78 / **−2.73** | **+0.083** | **+5.00 / +5.28** |
| twin B (n=123) | +0.044 | +1.50 / +3.90 | **+0.110** | **+2.86 / +6.06** |
| twin C (n=123) | −0.013 | +2.53 / **−4.16** | **+0.070** | **+4.43 / +0.80** |
| **v10 engine EF (n=213)** | +0.046 | +13.17 / −3.43 | **−0.037** | +0.40 / −5.26 |

**Four of five improve, and all four go both-halves-positive from a negative second half.** That is
genuinely novel: every other construction I have tested today — the on/off gate, the stake modifier,
the trend guard — made the second half *worse*. This one fixes it.

**Why it is still only a candidate:**

1. **One set contradicts** — the v10 engine gets worse (+0.046 → −0.037), and its cheapest quartile
   (+0.109/fire) outperforms its dearest (+0.027), the reverse of the others.
2. **The sweep is non-monotone with a peak at 0.48** — 0.45, then 0.48 best, then 0.50 dips, then
   0.55 rises again. That is the spike shape I used to condemn the trend guard and then my own entry
   cap. I am not going to make that mistake a third time in the same day.
3. **The five sets share candles**, so four agreeing is not four independent confirmations.

**Recommendation: shadow twin, not a dial.** Run an EF variant with a 0.48 ask floor beside the
baseline and judge it on the ledger's normal rules. It is the best candidate I have produced today,
and it still does not meet the bar for shipping.

## Interpretation

A high ask means the model was confident and the book agreed. That EF's expensive fires *also* pay
after the price is a calibration statement: when EF is sure and pays up, it is right often enough to
cover the price. Its cheap fires are where it is guessing. REVERSAL is the opposite because it is a
different bet — it profits from mispriced tails, not from confident agreement.
