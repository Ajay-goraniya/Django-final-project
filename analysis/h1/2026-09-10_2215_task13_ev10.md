does not apply. Instead: restrict A (baseline) and C (EV 1.0) to their **common candle window**

I agreed to strip on 09-10 cannot contribute. A 200 graded, C 180 graded; both fired 179, only A 21,
only C 1.

## The discrepancy — please reconcile

V's ledger (NOTES_v12, 17:31 and later) reports: *"Paired A vs C at common asks: decision edge +59.2,
halves +30.7/+28.5 (156 graded)."* I get **+2.62 at $1**, which is **+26.2 at $10** — still less than
half of +59.2, and mine rests on 30 decisions rather than 156.

Candidate explanations, in order of my suspicion:
1. **Different denominator of "decision".** If V scores C's *whole book* against A's whole book, the
   same-side candles contribute their PnL difference; at a common ask that must be zero, so any
   non-zero contribution there means the asks are not in fact common in V's version.
2. **Window.** My common window is 00:05–20:45 on 09-10 (200/180 graded). V's 156 graded suggests a
   different span.
3. **Stake.** $1 vs $10 explains a factor of 10 and no more.

I am not claiming V's number is wrong — V has the fresher process state and may be pairing on
something I cannot see from the snapshots. But **the two numbers cannot both describe the same
quantity**, and C is a leading candidate for v12, so it is worth ten minutes to settle which. If
mine is right, EV 1.0 is unproven rather than passing.

## What I would do

Leave C running — it costs nothing and it is not harming anything. But **do not promote EV 1.0 into
v12 on the strength of the paired number until the two measurements agree.** The trend guard is the
precedent: it "kept passing" on a headline figure that decomposed into a handful of decisions plus
noise, and the decomposition is what caught it.
