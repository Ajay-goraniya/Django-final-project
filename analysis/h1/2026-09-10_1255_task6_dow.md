# H1 — Task 6: weekday-to-weekend decay (and a mechanism for Task 3)

**Verdict: the user's hypothesis is not supported on the signal side. Directional edge does not
decay toward the weekend — it improves. But the improvement is not about the day: it is about
volatility, and that fact answers V's Task 3 question about why the rv60 gate reversed.**

## Data

Task 6 as written asks for day-of-week PnL on "the real historical set you used for Task 2".
**That set cannot answer it.** `poly_pnl` graded fires span only:

| date | day | graded fires |
|---|---|---|
| 2026-09-08 | Tue | 46 |
| 2026-09-09 | Wed | 136 |
| 2026-09-10 | Thu | 63 |

Three weekdays, **no weekend at all**, and two of three buckets are under the 60-fire floor. There
is no honest day-of-week answer in the fire data, and none of the committed twins reach further
back. So I pulled real market history instead:

**Binance spot BTCUSDT 1-second klines, 2026-07-01 to 2026-09-09 — 71 days, 10 weekends, 20,448
five-minute candles (20,317 graded, i.e. close != open), 6.1 M per-second observations.**
Downloaded from `data.binance.vision`, stored in scratch, not committed. Builder:
`analysis/h1/build_paths.py`. Note the futures endpoint has no 1s klines; spot does, and it is the
same instrument the engine's spot path watches.

This measures **the market**, not the model: sign persistence inside the candle, which is the raw
material EF's directional call is made of. It does not include quotes, fills or fees. So it answers
Task 6(b) — *"does the signal itself degrade, or only the fills/quotes?"* — for the signal half, and
leaves the fills half unanswerable until the fire record spans a weekend.

thing to act on. I would log it and re-check, not gate on it.

## The real driver: volatility, not the day

Splitting the same 20,317 candles by full-candle range instead of by day:

| range quartile | n | **t=20s** | t=60s | crossings | weekday / weekend |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Q1 quietest (≤5.4 bps) | 5080 | **61.0%** | 65.1% | 2.46 | 2130 / **2950** |
| Q2 (5.4-9.4) | 5079 | 60.1% | 65.1% | 3.12 | 3721 / 1358 |
| Q3 (9.4-15.3) | 5079 | 58.9% | 64.9% | 3.46 | 4265 / 814 |
| Q4 busiest (>15.3) | 5079 | **57.3%** | 65.3% | 3.91 | 4548 / **531** |

Early directional persistence falls monotonically as the candle gets busier — 61.0% to 57.3% — and
**weekends are overwhelmingly the quiet quartile** (2950 of the 5080 quietest candles). So
"weekends are better" is "quiet candles are better", and weekends are quiet. The day of week is a
proxy; volatility is the variable.

## This answers V's Task 3 question

V asked: *"does the effect depend on fire second (v10 ~65 s vs v11 ~20 s)?"* **Yes, decisively —
and it is the whole story.** Look down the two accuracy columns above:

- **At t=20 s, accuracy varies with volatility: 61.0% -> 57.3% across the quartiles.**
- **At t=60 s it is flat: 65.1 / 65.1 / 64.9 / 65.3 — no dependence on volatility at all.**

A volatility gate is therefore a different instrument for a lane that fires at 20 s than for one
that fires at 65 s. On the v10 path (~65 s) it is acting on a relationship that does not exist at
that horizon, so whatever it produced there was fitted to something else. On the v11 path (~20 s)
volatility genuinely matters — but **in the opposite direction to my Task 2(b) result**: the quiet
candles are the predictable ones, so a gate that *keeps* high-vol fires (`rv60 >= 0.3`) is keeping
the fires whose direction is hardest to read.

That is consistent with V's refutation on the v11 path, and it is the mechanism behind it. **I
withdraw the `rv60 >= 0.3` recommendation for the v11 path.** If a vol gate belongs on v11 at all,
this data says test the *inverse* — skip the busiest candles, not the quietest — and test it on the
v11 fires, not on mine. Note the direction also matters for how it interacts with price: quiet
candles sit near the open, so their asks sit near 0.50, and cheap-entry PnL and directional accuracy
are not the same objective. That trade-off needs the fire data to settle; the market data cannot.

## What I would tell the user

Their recollection that earlier models decayed toward the weekend is real in the sense that
**weekend tape is a different regime** — half the range, a third fewer reversals. A model tuned on
weekday volatility can easily bleed there without the *signal* being worse, because sizing, quote
spreads and EV thresholds calibrated on 14 bps candles are wrong on 7 bps ones. On this evidence,
the place to look is the fills and the EV calibration, not the direction model.

## Sample sizes and caveats

20,317 graded candles, ~2,800-3,200 per weekday, 5,653 weekend, 71 consecutive days, both halves
reported. Real 1s klines only — no synthetic paths, no resampling. Limits: this is one instrument
over one 10-week window; it measures sign persistence, not tradeable PnL (no quotes, spread, fee or
fill model); "t=20s" is a clean proxy for EF's fire, but the real lane fires on a model output at a
variable second, not on the raw sign. The fills-side half of Task 6 stays open until the fire
record covers a weekend — worth noting that **the first weekend after 09-10 is the earliest this
can be answered live**, so it is a reason to keep the twins running through it.
