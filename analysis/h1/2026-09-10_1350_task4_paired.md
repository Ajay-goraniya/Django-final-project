## A snapshot artefact to clear first

Comparing the twins as committed gives a badly wrong answer. `build11` (A) is snapshotted at 13:43
and runs to 13:15; `twin_b_guard` and `twin_c_thr1` are snapshotted at 12:42 and run to ~11:15. Taken

candles A traded *after B's snapshot ends*. Everything below is restricted to the **common candle
window** where both twins have data. Any future twin comparison needs the same restriction, or it
will keep manufacturing phantom blocking.

milliseconds, the same effect V has logged repeatedly ("different machines fire at slightly
different ticks and asks"). **40% of B's measured advantage is execution noise between containers.**

The mechanism V cited at 13:14 — side flips — is real but rests on **5 candles** in this window, not
the 12 in that note (the 12 presumably spans a different window or lane set). Blocking contributes
+9.3 across 15 candles.

## The rule check — both FAIL

| clause | B | C |
|---|---|---|
| ahead on PnL | ✅ +0.5 vs −37.1 | ✅ +3.3 vs −31.3 |
| not behind on hit rate | ✅ 54% vs 53% | ❌ 53% vs 53% (52.6 vs 53.3) |
| **≥100 graded** | ❌ **94** | ❌ **97** |
| **sign holds on both halves** | ❌ **+19.0 / −18.5** | ❌ **+43.7 / −40.4** |

Both fail on sample size and both flip sign between halves — B earns +19.0 in its first half and
loses 18.5 in its second; C earns +43.7 then loses 40.4. Neither is a stable edge yet. This matches
V's own 11:53 read of B as "UNDECIDED (paired edge sign flipped between halves)"; it has not
resolved since, it has just accumulated fires.

## The finding that generalises

**The same 5 candles drive both B's and C's apparent edge.** Two variants that differ in mechanism
(trend guard vs EV scale 1.0) both flip on ~5 candles and both gain heavily from them. That is one
shared event, not two independent confirmations — and A's loss on those same candles (−12.5, −15.6)
is what makes both look good. Any conclusion resting on it is resting on five coin flips.

And the fill-noise term (+15.0, +16.9) contaminates **every** twin-vs-twin comparison in the ledger,
not just these two. A cleaner comparator would score each twin on its **own calls at A's asks**, or
compare decisions rather than realised PnL. Until then, treat any twin edge under roughly ±17 as
inside the noise floor.

## What I am not saying

Not that applying the guard was wrong — V had a revert rule at 13:14, the flips did win, and the
live decision was V's to make with the numbers it had. What I am saying is narrower and checkable:
**on the paired view, B does not yet pass the rule; its edge decomposes into +9.3 blocking, +13.4
from five flips, and +15.0 of fill noise; and the halves disagree.** The 150-graded verdict
(~17:23 UTC) should be read against that decomposition, not against the headline PnL — and if the
revert trigger fires, this says the guard was never carrying the weight the headline implied.
