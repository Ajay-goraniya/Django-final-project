hold only a handful of fills each - a 100% recovery rate on 3 fills is not a 100% recovery rate.
Fills also cluster: 15 of them sit in the single 17:00 candle of 09-30, so the effective number of
independent observations is nearer 24 than 43.

E. THE ONE THING THESE GRIDS SAY MOST CLEARLY
  The LOST column is 4 in every single bucket, percent or points, from -10% all the way to -50%. That
  is not a coincidence: all four losing fills dropped by at least half, so every loss had a deep
  drawdown - and yet 17 of the 21 fills that halved still WON. A deep drawdown is therefore NECESSARY
  but nowhere near SUFFICIENT for a loss in this sample. Reading it the other way round - "it fell 50%,
  cut it" - would have closed 17 winners to avoid 4 losers.
  And since only 2 of those 21 minima printed in the last 30 s, the dips are overwhelmingly real
  intra-candle moves rather than the settlement arriving, so the recovery is genuine recovery.
  WHAT THIS DOES NOT SAY: it does not say a stop-loss would lose money, because the probe holds to
  settlement and has no exit - these are counterfactual paths, not trades. It says only that on 43
  graded fills over 24 candles, price falling hard after our fill carried almost no information about
  the outcome.

THREE THINGS I CHECKED BEFORE BELIEVING THIS TABLE
 1. THE SAMPLE GREW WHILE I WORKED. V asked about "the 43 graded fills over 23 candles"; the 20:30
    fill settled in the meantime, so the table above is 44 GRADED over 24 CANDLES. Same method, one
    more fill. I am not relabelling it 43 to match the brief.
 2. THE "all" AND "maker-bid" ROWS ARE IDENTICAL, and identical numbers across two arms is exactly the
    thing I have been wrong about before, so I tested it rather than shipping it. The two maxima differ
    on only 4 of 44 fills, and on NONE of those does the difference cross one of the four levels - so
    the identity is structural, not a copied query. Near resolution a 5-minute token's bids and trades
    both sit at 0.99, which is why they coincide.
 3. EVERY "reached" BUCKET HAS ZERO LOSSES, which looked wrong until I tested the obvious explanation:
      level 0.80  reached n=39 -> 39 WON, 0 LOST | NOT reached n=4 -> 0 WON, 4 LOST
      level 0.95  reached n=40 -> 40 WON, 0 LOST | NOT reached n=4 -> 0 WON, 4 LOST
    That is PERFECT SEPARATION. Reaching 0.80+ after our fill is, in this sample, the same event as
    winning - a 5-minute binary trading at 0.80 is already resolving.

SO THE ANSWER TO THE OWNER'S QUESTION IS NOT "WHICH LEVEL IS BEST"
Taking profit LOSES at every level, and loses MOST at the level people reach for first:
    0.80  TP +21.83 vs HOLD +54.14  = -32.31
    0.85  TP +30.16 vs HOLD +55.14  = -24.98
    0.90  TP +38.48 vs HOLD +55.14  = -16.65
    0.95  TP +46.81 vs HOLD +55.14  =  -8.33
Excluding the 09-30 17:00 candle (15 fills) the shape is identical: -19.31 / -15.23 / -10.15 / -5.08.
The reason is point 3. A take-profit here cannot avoid losers, because no loser ever gets near these
levels; all it can do is cap winners. The closer the level is to 1.00 the less it costs, converging on
holding - which is the same statement as "do not do it".
TWO WAYS THIS IS OPTIMISTIC AND STILL LOSES: no sell fee is charged (a real exit crosses the spread and
pays the taker fee) and each fill is sold at its own size with no slippage. Both flatter the TP column.
