WHAT THIS ANSWERS
  1. NO fixed15 cell survived the train filter, in ANY session. fixed15/US 13-20 had train +75.7 on n=40
     but its halves were +112.7 / -37.0, so the both-halves rule dropped it. And fixed15
     trading ALL sessions is the BEST arm on the sealed test (+86.8, n=98, 2/3 days). So for fixed15 the session
     split found nothing - applying it would have taken us from the best result on the board to no trades.
  2. For the RAW arms the split is the difference between a loss and a gain. All sessions: raw25 S0=60
     -116.7 (n=132, DD 175.8) and raw25 S0=0 -148.4 (n=156, DD 207.1), both losing on
     EVERY test day (0/3 and 0/3). Their kept session cells: +26.3 (n=39) and +34.6 (n=10).
     The effect is large and points the same way in both raw variants, which is the one thing here
     that is not explainable by a single lucky cell.
  3. The EF-10 cells are the CONTROL, and they say be careful: the same procedure kept three US cells
     whose sealed test is -0.5, -0.5, +0.6 - indistinguishable from zero. A selection rule that
     returns a real number on one arm and 0.0 on another, from the same train window, is not yet a finding.
  4. Sample. The pooled +60.5 leans on raw25 S0=0 / Late 20-24 at n=10 fills (09-28 +44.9, 09-29 -10.3).
     Under 60 graded fires is 'insufficient' by our own standard - do not read it. raw25 S0=60 / US 13-20
     at n=39 is the only kept cell with both a real effect and a real sample, and it is still under 60
     and still only 2 days. No cell in this study clears the 60-fill bar.

VERDICT: session timing DOES separate the raw arms - US and Late carry them, Asia and Europe sink them -
but it does NOT beat simply using fixed15 unfiltered, and the EF-10 control shows the same selection
procedure returning zero. NO DEPLOY, NO CHANGE: this is a research answer to a research question.
