M35 (1) VERDICT - TWO FINDINGS, AND THE FIRST ONE IS A SPEC BUG NOT A RESULT.

1) THE RULE AS LITERALLY WRITTEN IS DEGENERATE. With the stop sitting at P from second one and the
trigger being the BID, it fires immediately on essentially every trade - 63 of 63 ask-touch entries
and 121 of 125 taker entries stop out, nearly all at exactly P. The reason is mechanical and not a
market fact: we enter at the ASK (or at our own resting bid, in which case the best bid IS our
price), so bid <= P already holds by the width of the spread before anything happens. "RATCHET as
written" is therefore not a strategy, it is "do not trade" - +9.00 on 63 trades with std 0.35.
The owner's words ("book moves 10% up, set stop loss at 5% below ... if price drops we sell at
marked point") only make sense if the mark-stop is ARMED by the first ratchet step, so that is what
the ARMED rows test, and those are the rows to read.

2) THE ARMED RATCHET IS THE FIRST OF THE OWNER'S IDEAS THAT IMPROVES EVERY RISK NUMBER WITHOUT
BREAKING ANYTHING. On the taker entries (n 125, the usable sample):
                                 $@10     per $1    maxDD    std   green
  HOLD to settlement           -56.41    -0.0451  -131.91  10.17    3/6
  ARMED ratchet, 0 fee          +68.02   +0.0544   -47.53   5.84    4/6
  ARMED ratchet, taker fee      +40.94   +0.0328   -49.25   5.85    4/6
  ARMED ratchet, sell at BID     -7.18   -0.0057   -59.69   5.92    3/6
Drawdown cut by about two thirds, per-trade std almost halved, more green days, and the loss floor
intact at -10.00 because the rule never re-enters - which is exactly what M32's rule broke. It beats
HOLD on per $1 in all three exit treatments. Halves on the zero-fee column: +0.1081 then -0.0001,
so positive then flat rather than positive then negative.

AND HERE IS THE HONEST LIMIT, WHICH IS LARGE: the entire apparent profit is the difference between
selling AT the stop and selling at the BID THAT TRIGGERED IT. At the stop it is +0.0544 per $1; at
the actual triggering bid it is -0.0057, i.e. flat. A resting sell at the stop fills only if someone
takes it, and the bid that trips us is by definition at or below the stop - so the realistic number
is the -0.0057 one. Read that way the armed ratchet does not make money; it takes a -0.0451 book to
roughly zero while cutting the drawdown by two thirds. That is a RISK result, not a profit result,
and it is the first time one of the owner's ideas has produced one.
SAMPLE: n 125 taker / 63 ask-touch over SIX days (09-11..09-16 is the only part of the M29 window
with a real bid), and the ask-touch arm clears the 60 bar by three. On the ask-touch entries both
HOLD (-0.2482) and the armed ratchet (-0.1691) are deeply negative - the ratchet improves them by
0.08 per $1 and still cannot rescue an adversely selected entry.
London's real fixed-EF fills - part (2) - is the test that matters, because there the entry prices,
the book and the fills are all facts. Nothing live touched here.

M35 STEP 5 CORRECTED (owner amendment 14b4c9c) BESIDE VERSION 1. READ-ONLY. 2026-10-03 11:19:08 UTC
CORRECTED: a stop at S becomes the NEW MARK; re-buy at the ask once the price comes back up
through S to >= 1.10*S; ratchet then runs on S. First stop arms only once the bid reaches P.
KEY DIFFERENCE FROM M32: the mark ratchets DOWN to each stop, so each re-entry is CHEAPER than
the last (1.10*S, not 1.10*P). M32 paid 10% over the ORIGINAL mark every cycle, which is what
guaranteed its loss. That mechanism is absent here - but it still re-enters, so the loss floor
can break; the worst-trade column is where to check that.

CORRECTED STEP 5 VERDICT - THE BEST-LOOKING CELL IN THE WHOLE PROJECT, AND ONE ASSUMPTION CARRIES IT.

THE OWNER'S CORRECTION IS A REAL IMPROVEMENT ON HIS OWN EARLIER RULE, AND THE REASON IS STRUCTURAL:
because the mark ratchets DOWN to each stop, re-entry costs 1.10*S and not 1.10*P. M32 paid 10% over
the ORIGINAL mark on every cycle, which guaranteed its loss arithmetically. That is gone. On the
taker entries the corrected rule beats version 1 and HOLD on every money column:

Six green days out of six, both halves positive, drawdown a quarter of HOLD's. Nothing else tested
for this owner has done that.

BUT THE PRIMARY EXECUTION HE ASKED FOR IS THE RESTING ENTRY, AND THERE IT IS FLAT: ask-touch entries
give +0.0013 per $1 at zero fee, -0.1322 with the exit fee and -0.1603 selling at the bid. The
good numbers above are the TAKER column, which is the secondary one. On his own primary the rule
roughly breaks even at best.

AND THE ASSUMPTION DOING THE WORK IS NOW DOING FAR MORE OF IT THAN IN VERSION 1. The corrected rule
takes 224 stops and 170 re-buys across 125 entries - version 1 took 83 stops and never re-entered.
Every one of those 224 exits is assumed to fill EXACTLY AT THE STOP, when a resting sell only fills
if someone takes it and the bid that trips it is by definition at or below it. Replace that single
assumption with the actual triggering bid and +0.1339 becomes -0.0216. The entire difference between
the best cell in this project and a losing one is one fill assumption applied 224 times.
THE LOSS FLOOR IS ALSO BROKEN AGAIN, as it must be for anything that re-enters: worst single trade
-16.31 at zero fee, -26.68 selling at the bid, against HOLD's exact -10.00.

WHAT I WOULD SAY TO THE OWNER: his correction fixed the thing that was mathematically wrong with his
first rule, and this is the first version of his idea that is not self-defeating. Whether it makes
money depends entirely on whether a resting sell at the stop actually fills - which this box cannot
answer, because it has no record of our own resting orders being taken. London's real fixed-EF fills
with its own book is the only place that question can be settled, and it now has a specific thing to
measure: of the stops this rule would have placed, what fraction filled at the stop rather than
worse. Nothing here licenses a live change, and nothing was changed.
