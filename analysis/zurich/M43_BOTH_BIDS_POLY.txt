THE ANSWER TO THE OWNER'S QUESTION IS IN ONE NUMBER: THE LEG WE ARE LEFT HOLDING WINS 0.0-0.1% OF
THE TIME. At b=0.30 there are 1,653 one-sided fills and 0.1% of them win; from b=0.40 upward it is
literally 0.0% - not one stranded leg ever won. Expected value of a one-sided fill is -b almost
exactly, i.e. a total loss of the premium paid.
The reason is structural, not statistical: for an ask to fall from ~0.50 to 0.30-0.48 on one side,
that side has to be ON ITS WAY TO ZERO. The market only sells you a 5-minute binary at 0.30 when it
is already pricing that side as the loser, and it settles at 0. So a one-sided fill is not a half-
finished arbitrage, it is the market handing you the losing ticket - reliably, 999 times in 1,000.

THE BREAK-EVEN IS A CLOSED FORM AND IT IS NEVER MET. With neither-fill rare, the rule breaks even
when both% x (1-2b) = one% x b, i.e. when both% = b/(1-b). Required against actual:
  b=0.30  need 42.9%  got 34.7%     b=0.40  need 66.7%  got 53.7%
  b=0.34  need 51.5%  got 42.3%     b=0.44  need 78.6%  got 62.2%
  b=0.38  need 61.3%  got 49.8%     b=0.48  need 92.3%  got 71.0%
Both-fill frequency does rise with b - 34.7% to 71.0% - but the required frequency rises faster,
so the gap widens at every step. That is why the grid has no optimum: the rule is further from
working the more aggressively you price it.
