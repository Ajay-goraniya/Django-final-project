M44 VERDICT: EVERY CELL LOSES WITH FEES, AND THE ZERO-FEE COLUMN SAYS WHY - THE 0.60 TRIGGER HAS
NO EDGE AT ALL, SO THERE IS NOTHING FOR A STOP TO PROTECT.

WITH THE REAL TAKER FEE, which is the only column that describes a trade you could place:
  TRAIL R0  -0.0448 per candle  t -10.50  green 1/12   buys/candle 1.56
  OWN   R0  -0.0467             t -10.27  green 1/12   buys/candle 1.56
  TRAIL R1  -0.1956             t -17.67  green 0/12   buys/candle 5.98
  OWN   R1  -0.1828             t -16.41  green 0/12   buys/candle 5.49
No session is positive in any of them (best is EU at -0.036, worst US at -0.053 on TRAIL R0).

THE ZERO-FEE COLUMN IS THE DIAGNOSIS: TRAIL R0 is +0.0022 per candle at t +0.54 and OWN R0 is
-0.0000 at t -0.00. That is break-even to four decimal places on 2,547 candles. Buying a 5-minute
binary at 0.60 the moment it reaches 0.60 is a FAIRLY PRICED bet - the market's 0.60 is right - so
the entry contributes nothing, positive or negative, and the entire loss in the fee column is the
fee. One entry at 0.60 costs 0.07*0.6*0.4 = 0.0168 in fee, and at 1.56 buys a candle plus the exit
fee that is about 0.047 - which is exactly the gap between +0.0022 and -0.0448.
So the answer to the owner is not "your stop is wrong". It is that there is no edge at the entry for
any exit rule to work on: a stop can only protect profit that the entry generates, and at 0.60 the
entry generates none.

RE-BUYING IS THE ONE THING THAT IS ACTIVELY WRONG, AND IT IS NOT JUST THE FEE. R1 is negative even
at ZERO fee - TRAIL R1 -0.0269 at t -3.04, OWN R1 -0.0242 at t -2.70 - so re-entering at 0.60 after
being stopped out is a losing decision in its own right, before any cost. It also quadruples the
turnover (1.56 to 5.98 buys a candle), which is why it is four times worse once fees are charged.
That is the same conclusion M32, M36 and M42 reached by three other routes: re-entry is where this
family of ideas loses its money.
HIS % RATCHET IS NOT THE PROBLEM EITHER, and he should hear that: OWN tracks TRAIL almost exactly
(-0.0467 vs -0.0448 with fees, -0.0000 vs +0.0022 without). His ratchet is neither better nor worse
than the points trail - both are managing a position that has no expected profit to manage.

LIMIT = resting buy at 0.60, maker, fills ONLY if the first ask to reach 0.60 is <= 0.61 (the

VERDICT: THE OWNER IS RIGHT THAT A RESTING ENTRY IS MUCH BETTER - AND THE RULE STILL LOSES, BECAUSE
THE STOP CANNOT BE RESTING. THE CRUX IS THE AVERAGE EXIT BID: 0.4610, NOT 0.5000.

  IDEAL  +0.0668 per buy, t +17.80, 12 of 12 green days, exit bid 0.5000 by assumption
  LIMIT  -0.0070 per buy, t  -1.17,  4 of 12 green days, exit bid 0.4610
  TAKER  -0.0255 per buy, t  -6.06,  1 of 12 green days, exit bid 0.4577
As imagined the rule is strongly profitable - +0.0668 a buy with every one of the 12 days green and
t +17.8. That is not noise; buying the favourite at 0.60 and risking exactly 10 cents to make 40 IS
a good bet. The problem is that one of those two numbers is not available.

WHERE THE +0.0668 GOES, in order of size:
 1. STOP SLIPPAGE, the whole crux: when the 0.50 stop trips we sell at an average bid of 0.4610,
    3.9 cents BELOW the stop, and 73.1% of buys stop out - so slippage alone costs about
    0.731 x 0.039 = 0.029 per buy. Polymarket has no stop order: a sell placed under the bid is a
    taker sell at whatever the bid is, and the bid is below 0.50 precisely because the side is
    falling. The 10-cent risk the owner is pricing is really a 14-cent risk.
 2. THE EXIT FEE on that taker sell, 0.07p(1-p) at p~0.46, about 0.017 per stop = 0.012 per buy.
 3. THE ENTRY, and this is the part where he is RIGHT: LIMIT pays nothing to enter and TAKER pays
    the fee plus whatever the ask is above 0.60, which is worth 0.0185 per buy (-0.0070 against
    -0.0255). Resting the entry recovers almost two cents a buy. It is the single biggest
    improvement anyone has produced on this family - it just is not two cents short, it is seven.
Together: +0.0668 ideal, minus ~0.029 slippage, minus ~0.012 exit fee, lands on the LIMIT row's
-0.0070. The arithmetic closes, so this is a cost accounting rather than a statistical result.

AND THE RESTING ENTRY HAS ITS OWN PRICE, WHICH SHOULD NOT BE HIDDEN: the LIMIT row fills on only
1,772 of the 3,966 trigger events, 45%. The other 55% of the time the ask jumps from under 0.60
straight past 0.61 and our resting order is never touched. So a resting buy at 0.60 gets filled
less than half the time it looks like it should - and it is the fast moves it misses.
SESSION DETAIL: ASIA is the one positive LIMIT cell (+0.0103 per buy, exit bid 0.455), EU -0.0127,
US -0.0110, LATE -0.0208. ASIA is the thinnest session and one positive cell out of four at this
strength is not a finding; it is in the table so the owner can see it rather than be told it is not
there. Every TAKER cell is negative.
WHAT WOULD CHANGE THE ANSWER: not a different stop level - a way to EXIT at a resting price. Every
cent recovered on the exit bid is worth about 0.0073 per buy, so closing even half the 3.9-cent gap
would put this family at break-even. That is a venue-mechanics question (post a sell above the
falling bid and hope it fills) and this box cannot answer it: it has no record of our own resting
sells being taken. Nothing live was touched.
