M33 VERDICT: THE FILTER DOES NOT SURVIVE SCALE. 14 of 15 cells FAIL, and W=10 - the actual
hypothesis - fails on ALL FIVE arms.

ON FAV CALM, THE ARM THAT MATTERS, W=10 IS NOT WEAK, IT IS BACKWARDS:
  UNFILTERED  1771 fires  +382.84  +0.0216/$1  maxDD -148.70  11/19 green
  W=10 KEPT    963 fires   +71.89  +0.0075/$1  maxDD -166.57   8/19 green
  W=10 REMOVED 808 fires  +310.95  +0.0385/$1  maxDD -111.18  15/19 green
The candles the filter THROWS AWAY return five times more per dollar than the ones it keeps, with a
smaller drawdown and 15 of 19 green days against 8. On 1,771 fires over 19 days the 10 s Binance
direction signal is not merely absent, it points the wrong way on this arm. M32's +0.18 vs +0.03
on 32 kept maker fills does not reproduce, and the honest reading is that M32 found noise in 32
observations - which is exactly what in-sample on n=32 is for finding.

THE ONE "PASS" IN THE GRID IS A SCORING ARTEFACT AND I AM NOT CLAIMING IT. FAV_mid at W=5 satisfies
every literal condition the prereg set - kept beats unfiltered, removed is negative, halves agree,
green days hold - but kept is -0.0421 per $1. The prereg's PASS test never required the kept arm to
be POSITIVE, so a filter can "pass" by losing slightly less on an arm that loses either way. That
is a defect in the bar, not a finding, and it should be fixed before the bar is used again.

WHAT IS REAL, AND IT IS NOT THE SAME CLAIM: on the two EF TAKER arms the filter does separate in the
right direction at every W, which is the opposite of what it does on FAV.
  EF fixed15 W=10: kept -0.0038 (n 298) vs unfiltered -0.0137, REMOVED -0.0592 (n 65)
  EF RAW25   W=10: kept -0.0195 (n 206) vs unfiltered -0.0555, REMOVED -0.1942 (n 50)
So on model-driven EF entries the removed group is clearly the worse group, and the filter lifts the
arm by about +0.01 to +0.04 per $1 - but it lifts it only from bad to roughly zero, never into
profit, and both arms fail the same-sign-both-halves test (each is positive in the first half and
negative in the second). A filter whose own direction of effect FLIPS between two datasets in the
same study - helpful on EF, harmful on FAV calm - has no claim to be a rule.

PRACTICAL CONCLUSION FOR THE OWNER'S "test on big scale": tested, at ~19 days and 1,771 FAV fires
plus 15 days and 569 EF fires. The 10 s filter is not a keeper. The closest thing to a signal in the
whole grid is FAV calm at W=30 (kept +0.0325 vs +0.0216, removed -0.0027, maxDD improved to -91.99),
which is the only cell where the kept arm is positive, beats unfiltered, and has a flat-to-negative
removed group - but it fails the green-day condition (10/19 vs 11/19), it is one cell out of fifteen
tried, and W=30 was not the hypothesis. Reporting it as a cell, not proposing it.
