Rule A turns a +0.077 per $1 book into -0.596 and flips 81W/27L into 43W/65L. It is worse in BOTH
halves (-202.65 then -27.72 against HOLD) so this is not one bad stretch.

WHY, AND THIS IS ARITHMETIC AND NOT A SAMPLE: the rule SELLS at the marked price P and RE-BUYS at
1.10*P. Every completed cycle therefore hands over at least 0.10*P per share plus two taker fees,
whatever the market then does. Median fill price is 0.66, so one cycle on 5 shares gives up at least
$0.33 before fees, and the rule ran 209 sells and 189 re-buys across 108 fills - at least $62 of the
loss is the cycle cost alone, by construction. The rest is selection: selling a winner at breakeven
forfeits the settlement payoff, and re-buying at 0.73 instead of 0.66 means the same correct side now
pays 10% more for the same $1. It fired on 56 of 108 fills (52%) and did nothing on the other 52,
which are identical to HOLD - so all the damage comes from the half it touched.
That matters for how this is reported back: the owner's rule is not a strategy that happened to lose
on a small sample. "Sell at P, re-buy at 1.10P" is a mechanism for repeatedly paying 10% to re-enter
the same position, and no amount of extra data will make it positive. It would need to be re-specified
to re-buy at or below the marked price, or to not sell at breakeven at all, before it is worth testing.

RULE B DOES NOT RESCUE IT, BUT ITS FILTER IS THE ONE THING HERE WORTH KEEPING. The 10 s Binance
direction filter made rule A less bad per dollar than the skipped set under rule A (-0.705 vs -0.551,
so not even that), but look at the HOLD column instead: the 32 fills it keeps returned +0.1821 per $1
held, against +0.0288 for the 52 it rejects on the real signal. That is a 6x difference in the right
direction on the question the owner actually asked ("so on a first buy we are sure that price will go
up most probably") - the answer is that the filter does pick better entries, it just must not be
combined with a rule that then sells them at breakeven.
CAVEATS, and they are heavy: n 32 against n 52, IN-SAMPLE on the same fills PROBE_EXIT_GRID was
fitted on, and the probe is stopped so this cannot be extended here. It is a hypothesis for London's
live fills out of sample, nothing more. The answer to "were the skipped ones the losers?" is NO -
